# Mold material and hardware data: notes and sources

Companion to `mold-materials.json` (same folder). The JSON is generated by
`gen/build_materials.py`. Every derived number (preload, torque, head bearing
pressure, thread engagement, thermal preload change, dowel fit loss) is computed
in that script, so change an input there and re-run `python -I build_materials.py`.

JSON top-level keys: `units`, `conventions`, `mold_materials` (16 grades),
`fasteners_common` (rules, thread engagement, thermal preload), `fasteners_inch`
(9 sizes), `fasteners_metric` (8 sizes), `inserts`, `dowels`, `stock_thickness`,
`hooks`.

Each material has a `confidence` field: `published`, `derived` or `estimate`.
Show estimates to the user as estimates.

## 1. Findings that change the design

1. **For aluminium, how long it sits at temperature matters as much as the
   temperature.** 6061-T6 loses nothing after 100 h at 150 C (MMPDS, cited in
   LIGO T1000564), but after 10,000 h it keeps only 78% of its yield (ASM
   long-term data). 7075-T6 keeps just **37%** after 10,000 h at 149 C
   (503 to 186 MPa). A mold held hot 8 h/day reaches 10,000 h in about 5 years,
   so the tool must use the `yield_long_10000h` row as the design basis. The
   `yield_short_0p5h` row only applies to the first heat-up.
2. **The aluminium choice depends on press temperature.**
   - At 100 C or below, any aluminium works, and 7075, QC-10 and Alumold 500
     are the strongest.
   - From 100 to 150 C, use 6061-T651 (derated to 0.78 x yield) or 5083. Do not
     use 7xxx alloys.
   - From 150 to 200 C (typical rubber and silicone cure), use steel (P20, 4140
     pre-hardened, H13). Aluminium is possible only as low-stress 5083 or MIC-6
     plates.
   - 2024-T351 keeps strength at 150 C only because it artificially ages, which
     changes its dimensions. Avoid it in hot molds.
3. **Steel bolts in hot aluminium gain preload.** Take an M10 12.9 screw
   tightened to 75% of proof, with a 50 mm grip in 6061, heated from 20 to
   150 C. Its preload rises from 42.2 to 54.9 kN, which is **98% of proof
   load** (`fasteners_common.thermal_preload.examples`). The aluminium under the
   head then creeps and relaxes. Recommendations:
   - Tighten to about 60-65% of proof.
   - Put hardened washers on all aluminium clamped faces.
   - Re-torque hot after 2-3 heat cycles.

   In P20 plates the same screw changes only about 4%.
4. **Head bearing pressure exceeds what aluminium can take.** A 12.9 SHCS at 75%
   of proof puts 313-551 MPa under the head (higher for M12 and up, because the
   counterbore clearance takes more of the head area). The allowable is about
   310 MPa for 6061 at 20 C and about 166 MPa for MIC-6. Use through-hardened
   washers (ASTM F436, ISO 7089 300HV or DIN 6340) on every aluminium face.
5. **Required thread engagement grows as the aluminium weakens with heat**
   (`thread_engagement.by_material`, rule Le/D >= 0.5 x Su_bolt / Su_parent):

   | Material | 20 C | 100 C | 150 C | 200 C |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 6061 | 2.0 D | 2.1 D | 2.6 D | 4.7 D |
   | 7075 | 1.1 D | 1.3 D | 2.9 D | 5.3 D |
   | MIC-6 | 3.7 D | | 4.5 D | |
   | 5083 | 2.1 D | | 2.8 D | |
   | Steel | 1.0 D | 1.0 D | 1.0 D | 1.0 D |

   A36 and 1018 need 1.5 D. When the result is above 2.5 D, use key-locking
   inserts (Keensert heavy duty), 2D Heli-Coils, or through-bolts with nuts.
6. **Steel dowels pressed into aluminium come loose when hot.** Diametral
   interference drops by (alpha_Al - alpha_st) x dT x d, which is about
   0.0016 x d from 20 to 150 C (0.016 mm on a 10 mm pin). That is the same size
   as a normal press fit. Two options:
   - Build in about 0.0016 x d of extra interference (the
     `press_hole_aluminium_150C_*` columns) and install by shrink fit.
   - Retain steel bushings with a flange and screws, or with Loctite 620
     (rated to 230 C).

   Use one round dowel plus one diamond pin or radial slot. Never use two tight
   round dowels across an aluminium/steel or hot/cold joint.
7. **Guide pins need a radial slot or a diamond bushing.** A 10 K temperature
   difference between lid and base, with 200 mm from the datum, gives 0.047 mm
   of mismatch. That is more than the 0.01-0.04 mm running clearance of a g6/H7
   guide. A steel lid on an aluminium base heated 130 K gives 0.28 mm. Use one
   round guide plus slotted or diamond bushings, and tapered interlocks for
   final registration.

How the other fields were built:

- **Elastic modulus vs temperature.**
  - Aluminium uses EN 1999-1-2 (E/E20 = 0.97, 0.93, 0.86, 0.78 at 100, 150,
    200, 250 C).
  - Tool steels use Uddeholm data (205 GPa falling to 200 GPa at 200 C).
  - Carbon steel uses EN 1993-1-2 kE, which is conservative.
- **Fatigue.**
  - Aluminium values are R = -1 smooth-specimen S-N values at 1e5, 1e6 and
    1e7 cycles. Aluminium has **no endurance limit**
    (`endurance_limit: false`).
  - The 5e8 values come from ASM / MatWeb: 6061 96.5, 7075 159, 2024 138 MPa.
  - Steel values are Shigley estimates: S'e = 0.5 Su (capped at 700 MPa),
    S(1e3) = 0.9 Su, log-log interpolation.
  - A press cycle is about R = 0, so convert with Goodman:
    sigma_max_allow = 2 / (1/Sf + 1/Su). Then apply Marin factors (machined
    surface ka of about 0.7-0.8 for steel, about 0.9 for aluminium), plus notch
    factors at bolt holes and cavity corners.
- **Machinability.** Each grade has three numbers:
  - `aa_rating`, an approximate Aluminum Association letter.
  - `index_1212`, on the AISI scale where 1212 = 100 (steels, annealed unless
    noted).
  - `rel_mrr_vs_P20`, a rough removal-rate multiple versus P20 pre-hardened.
    It is a heuristic for the future machinability MCP; replace it with the
    simulator's output.
- **Cost.** `cost_index_per_kg` is relative to 6061-T651 = 1.0 (about US$8/kg
  cut plate in 2025-26). `cost_index_per_volume` = index per kg x density/2700.
  These are order of magnitude only.
- **Grades worth adding later:** 2618-T61 and 2219-T87, the aluminium alloys
  made for elevated temperature (better than 6061 at 150-200 C), and Ampco /
  Moldmax beryllium-copper or copper-nickel for hot-spot inserts.

## 3. Fasteners

- **Inch, ASTM A574.**
  - Tensile stress areas and 75%-of-proof clamp loads are checked against the
    Fastenal "Torque-Tension Relationship for ASTM A574 Socket Head Cap Screws"
    (Rev 3-4-09). Examples: 1/4-20 at 3341 lb, 1/2-13 at 14,899 lb, 3/4-10 at
    33,864 lb.
  - Proof stress is 140 ksi up to 1/2 in and 135 ksi above (back-calculated from
    that chart). Minimum UTS is 180 / 170 ksi.
  - Torque uses T = K D F, with K = 0.15 lubricated, 0.16 as received, 0.20 dry.
    This matches Fastenal: 1/2-13 gives 93 / 99 / 124 ft-lb.
  - #10-24 and #10-32 are added with the same rule (As 0.0175 and 0.0200 in²).
- **Metric, ISO 4762 class 12.9.**
  - ISO 898-1 values: Sp 970, Rm 1220, Rp0.2 1100 MPa.
  - Proof loads: M5 13.8, M6 19.5, M8 35.5, M10 56.3, M12 81.8, M14 112,
    M16 152, M20 238 kN.
  - At 75% of proof with K = 0.20 the torques are M6 17.5, M8 42.6, M10 84.4,
    M12 147, M16 365 and M20 713 N·m. These are close to the VDI 2230 table
    values for mu = 0.12 at 90% yield utilisation (16.5, 40, 79, 136, 333 and
    649 N·m), which are listed for comparison and are approximate.
- **Dimensions** come from ASME B18.3 and ISO 4762 (head diameters),
  ASME B18.2.8 and ISO 273 (clearance holes), DIN 974-1 and common practice
  (counterbores), and 75%-thread tap drills.
- **Thermal preload** (formula in JSON):
  dF = (alpha_c - alpha_b) x dT x L / (L/(E_b A_b) + L/(E_c A_c,eq)).
  - A_c,eq is the VDI 2230 clamped-cone equivalent area. The example uses
    300 mm² for M10.
  - The bolt area is taken as half shank and half stress area.
  - E for aluminium is taken at 150 C.
  - For the real tool, compute A_c,eq from plate thickness and head diameter.
    Cooling below assembly temperature reduces preload by the same formula.
- **Thread-engagement rule, derived here:**
  - The internal-thread shear area per unit length is about 2.25 D. This was
    checked on 1/2-13 class 2B from the Machinery's Handbook / FED-STD-H28
    expression An = pi n Es,min [1/(2n) + 0.57735 (Es,min - En,max)], which gives
    0.715 pi D.
  - The parent material's shear strength is taken as 0.6 Su, and As is about
    0.57 D². That gives Le/D = 0.42 Su_b / Su_p, and a 1.2 margin gives
    0.5 Su_b / Su_p.
  - This sizes the joint so the bolt breaks before the thread strips, which is
    conservative.

## 4. Inserts

- **Helical wire inserts** (Heli-Coil, Recoil, Bollhoff): ASME B18.29.1 /
  DIN 8140. They need an STI tap, with tap depth = insert length plus 1/4 turn.
  - Length guide: 1 D light duty, 1.5 D standard, 2 D for aluminium.
  - Inch STI drills in the JSON: 13/64, 17/64, Q, X, 29/64, 17/32, 21/32 and
    25/32 for #10 to 3/4.
  - The Albany County Fasteners 2025 chart has misaligned rows (for example
    #3 for 1/4-20, which is wrong). Q for 5/16-18, Y for 3/8-16 and 17/32 for
    1/2-13 agree. Metric drill sizes are given as ranges, so the tool must use
    the chosen maker's table.
- **Key-locking inserts** (Keensert, sold by Clinserts and Bollhoff). For a
  1/4-20 internal thread:

  | Part | Duty | External thread | Tap drill | Countersink | Min depth |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | KN420J | Light | 3/8-16 | 0.332 in | 0.385 in | 0.43 in |
  | KNHL420J | Heavy, locking | 7/16-14 | 0.397 in | 0.447 in | |
  | KNHXH420J | Extra heavy | 1/2-13 | 0.453 in | | |

  Key-locking inserts use standard taps. A heavy-duty insert puts the parent
  shear diameter at about 1.75 D, so MIC-6 drops from 3.7 D required to about
  2.1 D.
- **Arconic's QC-10 guidance:** hardened steel inserts, sleeves and bushings at
  every bolt-down, dowel and nozzle/heater contact in aluminium mold plate.

## 5. Dowels and guides

- **Inch:** ASME B18.8.2 hardened ground dowels, standard size nominal +0.0002
  +/- 0.0001 in, oversize +0.001 in. Slip hole is nominal +0.0005/+0.0010 in.
  Press hole in steel is nominal -0.0003/0 in.
- **Metric:** ISO 8734 m6 pins (Misumi MSTH/MSTM m6, dual-fit p6/g6).
  - m6 deviations: 3-6 mm +4/+12 µm, 6-10 mm +6/+15 µm, 10-18 mm +7/+18 µm,
    18-30 mm +8/+21 µm (ISO 286).
  - In an H7 hole an m6 pin is only a transition fit. For a true press, use an
    N7/P7 hole or a p6 pin.
  - Slip holes: d +0.010/+0.025 mm up to 10 mm, then +0.015/+0.030 mm.
- **Guides:**
  - Leader pins: 1/2-2 in or 12-50 mm (DME, Progressive, HASCO Z00, Misumi GPA,
    Meusburger E1000).
  - Fit is g6/h6 pin in an H7 bushing.
  - Use 4 pins with one offset, and engage at least 1 D before the cavity
    closes.
  - Use bronze or hardened steel bushings in aluminium.

## 6. Stock thickness

The lists are in the JSON.

- **Rolled 6061/7075:** supplied over nominal, so the tool should plan to skim
  both faces and subtract about 0.03-0.06 in (0.8-1.5 mm) from nominal for
  finished thickness.
- **MIC-6:** supplied at finished decimal thickness +/-0.005 in with flatness
  of about 0.015 in, so no skim. Speedy Metals lists stock to 2.5 in; 3-4 in is
  by order.
- **P20:** sawn from distributor blocks, rough and decarburised; allow about
  1.5-3 mm per face. Standard mold-base plates (DME inch x.375/x.875 series,
  HASCO K-plates 22-196 mm) come ground and are often cheaper for standard
  footprints. These series are from memory and must be verified against current
  catalogs.
